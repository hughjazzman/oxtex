\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:app-results}

Solving for $(\alpha - \alpha_h)t > \log t$, 
for various values of $\Delta \alpha = \alpha - \alpha_h$ and $t$,
we see the values of $t$ for which the inequality holds in \autoref{tab:inequality}.
Of course, this does not take into account varying constant factors in the $O$ notation,
and the possibility of the actual overheads being closer to $O(t)$ or even $O(t^a)$ for some $0<a<3$ 
rather than $O(t^2)$ or $O(t^3)$.
Nevertheless, it showcases that as long as $\Delta \alpha > 0$, 
it will eventually outperform for a large enough $t$.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\Delta \alpha$ & 0.01 & 0.02 & 0.05 & 0.1  \\ \hline
$t >$ & 997 & 440 & 144 & 59  \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Values of $t$ for which $(\alpha - \alpha_h)t > \log t$ holds}
\label{tab:inequality}
\end{table}

The slope being close to 0 in \autoref{fig:logterms6-hs} points to a constant number of terms $O(1)$ 
with respect to the $T$-count.

showing that the heuristic is $poly(t)$ in time complexity.
